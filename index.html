About Me

I am an environmental and disaster sociologist and a doctoral student in the Department of Sociology at Virginia Tech. I am also an Assistant Professor of Sociology at Begum Rokeya University, Bangladesh, currently on study leave.

My research examines the intersections of environmental change, social inequality, institutional arrangements, and community resilience. I am particularly interested in understanding how social structures and institutions shape vulnerability, preparedness, anticipatory action, and recovery in communities exposed to environmental hazards and disasters.

Drawing on research in the United States and Bangladesh, I seek to advance sociological understandings of environmental problems and contribute to equitable and sustainable approaches to disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation.

Research Interests

Hazards and Disasters

Social vulnerability, disaster preparedness, psychosocial impacts, community resilience, and recovery.

Environmental Justice

Environmental inequality, industrial pollution, displacement, and unequal access to environmental resources.

Climate Change and Society

Climate adaptation, multidimensional poverty, institutional trust, and sustainable development.

Ongoing Research

Psychosocial Consequences of Hurricane Beryl

Examining resource loss, social inequality, and psychosocial outcomes among communities affected by Hurricane Beryl in the Houston metropolitan region.

Anticipatory Action in Bangladesh

Investigating how risk perception, early warning information, institutional trust, and social connectedness shape anticipatory behavior in disaster-prone communities.

Media Representation of Disasters

Exploring how news media construct narratives of vulnerability and resilience following disaster events.
